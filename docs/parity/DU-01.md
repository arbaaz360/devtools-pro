# DU-01 — Unix Timestamp Converter

`time.unix` parses one input (empty means "now") as ISO 8601, or as seconds/milliseconds with an `auto`/`seconds`/`milliseconds`/`iso` selector and a digit-count heuristic, and reports epoch, calendar and relative representations in UTC, plus a bounded `+ - * /` arithmetic grammar over numeric timestamps. It matches most of the card's Required and Acceptance list. Since #148 (AG-134, 2026-09-25) it also reports local time in the zone the clock carries (the machine's zone in the desktop). The remaining gap: it accepts no calendar date format other than ISO 8601 (ambiguous or word-based dates are rejected, not parsed).

## Gaps to close

- **Row 4 (missing): no common-date parsing beyond ISO 8601.** Closing this means picking a concrete second grammar (e.g. RFC 2822, or a short list of locale-agnostic formats) and adding it to `parseInput`, since today anything that is not the ISO regex or a bare arithmetic expression is rejected.

## Not covered

- Whether the interpretation selector, per-row copy buttons, reset control, and Tab-activation guard are actually wired up in the desktop shell — headless execution only proves the processor and manifest support them.
- Whether relative time visibly ticks while the tool is open (requires a live clock and the running app).
- Keyboard and selection behavior in the workspace.
- Any visual disagreement between displayed default bounds and calculated values (needs the rendered UI).
